Which print method suits your quantity?

The right method depends mostly on the number of pieces, the number of colours and the fabric. Here is the rule of thumb:

MethodWorth it fromFabricIdeal for
Heat transfer vinyl (HTV)1 pieceCotton, blends, polyesterNames, numbers, simple logos in 1 to 2 colours

What should you check before buying blanks?

  • Fabric: 100 % cotton is the all-rounder. Heather colours usually contain some polyester, which is fine for DTF and vinyl but less ideal for DTG.
  • Weight: light tees (approx. 145 gsm) feel airy, heavier ones (approx. 190 gsm and up) feel more premium and hold screen printing well. See heavy cotton T-shirts.
  • Side seams: tubular tees without side seams give an even print area.
  • Sizes: groups usually need XS to XXXL. How to split sizes is in the T-shirt size guide.
  • Total price and delivery: check VAT, delivery cost and, above all, whether the shop delivers to your country at all.

Which blank T-shirts are best for printing?
The easiest to print are T-shirts in 100 % cotton with a smooth surface and a weight of approx. 150 to 195 gsm. They take DTF, screen printing, DTG and vinyl well. Classic Fruit of the Loom tees are knitted as a tube with no side seams, so the print area stays even. For sublimation you need light polyester instead. More in blank T-shirts for printing.

From how many shirts is screen printing worth it?
As a rule of thumb from approx. 30 to 50 pieces per design. Screen printing needs a screen and set-up for every colour, and that cost is spread over the run. For smaller quantities DTF transfers or vinyl are usually cheaper and quicker. Always ask the printer for a total price including set-up. The full comparison is in screen printing vs DTF.
Can I use sublimation on cotton T-shirts?
No. Sublimation ink only bonds with polyester fibres, so on cotton the design looks faded and washes out quickly. Sublimation needs light garments with a high polyester content. For cotton tees choose DTF transfers, heat transfer vinyl, DTG or screen printing instead. The cotton blanks at Marmalade Co. are therefore not suitable for sublimation.
