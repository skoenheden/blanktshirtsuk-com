What is a blank hoodie?

A blank hoodie is a plain hooded sweatshirt with no print, logo or label on the outside. Printers, clubs, schools, small brands and event organisers buy them to add their own design by screen printing, DTF, vinyl or embroidery.

Most blank hoodies are made from sweat fleece: smooth on the outside, brushed and soft on the inside. The smooth face is what you print on, the brushed back is what keeps it warm.

What gsm is best for a blank hoodie?

Hoodies are much heavier than T-shirts. As a rough guide:

Pullover or zip hoodie: which should you choose?

  • Pullover hoodie: one smooth front with a kangaroo pocket. Best for a large chest print or a front and back design. The classic choice for leavers, teams and events.
  • Zip hoodie: the zip splits the front, so you print a small logo on the left chest and a big design on the back. Looks tidier as staff or workwear.
  • Crew neck sweatshirt: no hood and no pocket, so the largest clean print area and a lower price.

Many groups mix: pullover hoodies for members, zip hoodies for coaches or staff, both with the same back print.

Which print methods work on hoodies?

  • Screen printing: very durable on cotton-rich fleece and economical from approx. 30 to 50 pieces per design.
  • DTF: works on cotton, polyester and blends from 1 piece. Good for colourful designs in small runs.
  • Heat transfer vinyl: ideal for names and numbers. Cut mirrored, press at approx. 150 to 160 °C for 10 to 15 seconds, always following the manufacturer.
  • Embroidery: the heavier fleece carries stitches well, so a chest logo looks sharp and lasts.
  • DTG: possible, but the polyester share and the fleece texture make results less crisp than on a 100 % cotton tee.

Sublimation is not an option on cotton-rich hoodies. For a full method comparison see screen printing vs DTF.

Where should the print go on a hoodie?

Keep the design above the pocket seam on a pullover, usually 7 to 10 cm below the neckline. On the back, a design approx. 25 to 30 cm wide suits most adult sizes. Avoid the hood and the side seams, and press on a flat pad so the pocket does not create an uneven surface.

Popular placements for group hoodies:

  • Left chest logo: approx. 8 to 10 cm wide, ideal for embroidery or a small DTF print.
  • Large back print: the main design, often with a list of names for leavers or teams.
  • Sleeve print: a year, a web address or a small icon running down one arm.
  • Name on the hood or back neck: personalised with vinyl, one piece at a time.

Combining a small front logo with a big back print gives a professional look without making the hoodie stiff across the chest.

How do you wash a printed hoodie?

Wait approx. 24 hours after printing or pressing. Then wash inside out at 30 to 40 °C, without fabric softener, and let it dry flat or on a hanger instead of the tumble dryer. That protects both the print and the brushed inside. For sizing a group order, see the size guide.

Frequently asked questions

What gsm should a blank hoodie be?
For everyday merch approx. 240 to 280 gsm works well. Around 240 gsm feels lighter and suits layering or spring and autumn. Around 280 gsm feels warmer and more substantial and handles screen printing and embroidery well. Fruit of the Loom's Classic Hooded Sweat is approx. 280 gsm, the Lightweight Hooded Sweat approx. 240 gsm.
Can you use DTF on hoodies?
Yes. DTF transfers work on cotton, polyester and blends, so a typical 80/20 fleece is fine. Press on a flat area, avoid the pocket seam and the hood, and follow the transfer maker's settings. Pre-press the hoodie for a few seconds first to remove moisture. After pressing, wait approx. 24 hours before the first wash.
Pullover or zip hoodie for printing?
A pullover hoodie has an uninterrupted front, which is ideal for a large chest print. A zip hoodie splits the front, so designs are usually a small left chest logo or embroidery plus a big back print. Zip hoodies look smarter for staff wear, pullovers are the classic choice for clubs, schools and events.
