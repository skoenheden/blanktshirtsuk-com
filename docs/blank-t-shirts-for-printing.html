Blank T-shirts for printing: which blank suits which print method?

Updated 14 September 2026 · Prices checked 14 September 2026

Not every plain tee prints the same. Fabric, weight, surface and seams all change how a DTF transfer, a screen print or a vinyl name turns out. This guide matches blanks to print methods, lists what to check before you order and shows what a bulk order costs.

Quick answer

For DTF, screen printing, vinyl and DTG the safest blank is a 100 % cotton T-shirt of approx. 150 to 195 gsm with a smooth surface. Tubular tees without side seams print more evenly. Sublimation is the exception: it only works on light polyester. What makes a T-shirt good for printing?

A good print blank has three things going for it: a fabric the ink or transfer can bond to, a smooth and tightly knitted surface, and a shape that lies flat on a press. Brand names matter less than those basics.

For most people that means a 100 % cotton T-shirt in a mid weight. It takes almost every method, it is comfortable and it is widely available in many colours. Polyester only becomes the better choice when you want to sublimate.

What should you check before ordering blanks?

  1. Fibre content. Look for 100 % cotton if you plan DTG. Heather greys usually contain polyester.
  2. Weight (gsm). Approx. 145 gsm feels light and summery, approx. 165 is the everyday middle, approx. 195 feels sturdy. More in heavy cotton T-shirts.
  3. Seams. Tubular tees without side seams give an even print area.
  4. Colour range. For group orders you want the same colour in every size.
  5. Size range. Check that XS to XXXL is available and compare the chest measurements, see the T-shirt size guide.
  6. Total price and delivery. Is VAT included, what does delivery cost and does the shop ship to your country?

How do you prepare blanks for printing?

  • Do not pre-wash with fabric softener, it can stop inks and transfers from bonding.
  • Press the blank for a few seconds before applying DTF or vinyl to remove moisture and creases.
  • Test one shirt first, especially with a new transfer film or a new colour.
  • Follow the transfer manufacturer's temperature, time and pressure settings.

Afterwards, wait approx. 24 hours before washing. Wash inside out at 30 to 40 °C, skip the fabric softener and avoid the tumble dryer. Prints last noticeably longer that way.

Which colours print best?

White and light colours are the easiest: every method works and colours look true. Dark shirts need an opaque white underbase, which DTF and vinyl include by design, screen printing adds as an extra layer and DTG needs pretreatment for. Sublimation does not work on dark shirts at all.

For groups, pick one colour that works with your design and keep it the same across all sizes. That keeps the shirts looking uniform in photos. Order one or two spare blanks in the most common sizes, as they are handy for test prints and late sign-ups.

Frequently asked questions

What fabric is best for printing T-shirts?
100 % cotton is the most versatile choice. It works with DTF, screen printing, heat transfer vinyl and DTG, and it feels familiar to wear. Blends with some polyester are fine for DTF and vinyl but give weaker DTG results. Only sublimation needs polyester, ideally in a light colour, so cotton blanks are not suitable for that method.
Are side seams a problem when printing?
Not a big one, but tubular T-shirts without side seams are easier. The body is knitted as one tube, so there is no seam running under the edge of a wide design and the shirt lies flat on the press. Classic Fruit of the Loom tees are made this way. Fitted styles with side seams can still be printed, just keep the design away from the seams.

Do I need to wash blank T-shirts before printing?
Usually not. Pre-washing can leave softener residue that stops transfers and inks from bonding. It is better to press the blank for a few seconds to remove moisture and creases before applying DTF or vinyl. After printing, wait approx. 24 hours before the first wash, then wash inside out at 30 to 40 °C without fabric softener.
