Quick answer

T-shirt sizes are based on your chest measurement, taken around the fullest part of the chest. How do T-shirt sizes work?

T-shirt sizes are based mainly on the chest measurement of the body. The letter size (S, M, L) is just a label for a range of chest measurements, and every brand draws those ranges slightly differently. Comparing centimetres is the only reliable way to pick the right size across brands.

Length and sleeve length follow the chest size, so if the chest fits, the rest usually does too.

These are body measurements, not garment measurements. The shirt itself is wider to give ease of movement. Ladies' and fitted cuts use different charts, so check the product page.

How do you measure your chest correctly?

  1. Use a soft tape measure and wear a thin top or nothing at all.
  2. Wrap the tape around the fullest part of the chest, under the arms and across the shoulder blades. Keep it level.
  3. Read the measurement after breathing out normally. The tape should be snug, not tight.

No tape measure? Lay a T-shirt that fits well flat, measure straight across from armpit to armpit and double it. Subtract a few centimetres of ease and you have a rough body measurement.

What if you are between two sizes?

  • Choose the larger size if you like a relaxed fit or plan to wash warm.
  • Choose the smaller size only for a deliberately fitted look.
  • Consider the weight: heavier tees keep their shape better, lighter ones can feel closer to the body. More in heavy cotton T-shirts.

Cotton can shrink a little in the first washes. Wash inside out at 30 to 40 °C and avoid the tumble dryer to keep the fit.

How should you split sizes for a group order?

The best method is to ask. A simple form or chat poll with name and size avoids nearly all wrong sizes. If that is not possible, these splits are a sensible starting point for 50 shirts:

Order one or two extra pieces in M and L. They cover late sign-ups and misprints. Hoodies are usually worn looser, so many people pick the same size or one up; see blank hoodies.

How do you collect sizes without chaos?

Collecting sizes is where most group orders go wrong. A few simple habits help:

  • Share the chart first. Send the chest measurements with the sign-up form, not just the letters S to XXL.
  • Ask for one size per person in a single list, with name, size and colour if you offer a choice.
  • Set a deadline a few days before you need to order, and treat late entries as spares.
  • Offer a sample at training or in the office so people can try a size on.
  • Double-check totals per size before you order, then again before printing names or numbers.

For printed names, sort the list by size so the printer can match each name to the right shirt without guessing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure my T-shirt size?
Measure your body, not a shirt. Stand relaxed, breathe out normally and wrap a soft tape measure around the fullest part of your chest, under the arms and across the shoulder blades. Keep the tape level and snug but not tight. Compare the result in centimetres with the size chart and choose the larger size if you fall between two.
Do T-shirts run small or large?
It depends on the brand and cut. Classic unisex tees such as Fruit of the Loom's are cut fairly straight and generous, while fitted and ladies' cuts are narrower. Cotton can also shrink slightly in the first washes. Always compare chest measurements rather than the letter size, and choose one size up if you prefer a relaxed fit.
What size split should I order for a group?
If you cannot ask everyone, start with approx. 10 % S, 30 % M, 30 % L, 20 % XL and 10 % XXL for mixed adult groups. Shift towards S and M for younger or mostly female groups, and towards L to XXL for sports teams and work crews. Asking people for their size with a simple form is always more accurate.
Are UK T-shirt sizes different from EU sizes?
Letter sizes like S, M and L are used across the UK and EU, but each brand defines them with its own measurements. That is why the chest measurement in centimetres is the reliable reference.
