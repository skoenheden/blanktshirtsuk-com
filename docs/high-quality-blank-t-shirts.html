What makes a blank T-shirt "high quality" for printing?

For printing, quality means three things: the fabric takes ink or transfers well, the shirt keeps its shape after washing, and every piece in the order looks the same. A blank can feel soft in the shop and still fail on all three. That is why printers judge blanks on specs, not on the label.

The good news is that most quality markers are visible before you buy. Fibre content, weight and construction are usually listed on the product page, and the rest you can feel in one sample.

Does a heavier T-shirt mean better quality?

Not automatically. A heavier tee feels more substantial, is less see-through and gives screen printers a stable surface, so heavy blanks are popular for merch. But a well-knitted mid-weight tee can outlast a loosely knitted heavy one. Weight is one check out of seven.

  • Approx. 145 to 150 gsm: light and cool, good for summer events and DTF.
  • Approx. 165 gsm: the everyday middle, a safe default for most groups.
  • Approx. 180 to 195 gsm: sturdy, opaque, good for screen printing and premium merch.

The full weight comparison is in heavy cotton T-shirts.

Why do printers prefer tubular tees?

A tubular T-shirt is knitted as one round tube, so there are no side seams. The shirt lies flat on the press, a wide design does not cross a seam, and the body is less likely to twist sideways after washing. Classic Fruit of the Loom T-shirts are made this way, which is one reason they are among the most common print blanks in Europe.

Fitted fashion tees with side seams can still be printed well. Just keep large designs away from the seams and test one piece first.

How do you check quality before a big order?

  1. Order a small pack first in the colour and sizes you need most.
  2. Print one shirt with your real design and method.
  3. Wash it three times inside out at 30 to 40 °C without fabric softener.
  4. Check the result: print edges, collar shape, body twist and length.
  5. Compare sizes: lay S, M and L on top of each other to see that the grading is even.

If the test shirt still looks right after three washes, the blank is good enough for the full run. Choosing between print methods? See screen printing vs DTF vs DTG.

Frequently asked questions

What gsm is a good quality T-shirt for printing?
Approx. 150 to 195 gsm covers most print jobs. Around 150 gsm feels light and suits summer events and DTF, approx. 165 gsm is the everyday middle, and approx. 180 to 195 gsm feels sturdy, is less see-through and suits screen printing and merch. Weight alone does not make a shirt good, the knit and fibre matter just as much.
Is 100 % cotton better than a blend for printing?
For DTG, 100 % cotton gives the most reliable result because the ink bonds to cotton fibres. For DTF, screen printing and vinyl, cotton-rich blends work well too and can shrink a little less. Only sublimation needs polyester. If you print with several methods, 100 % cotton is the safest all-round choice.
Do expensive blanks always print better?
No. A higher price often pays for a fashion cut, a softer hand feel or a brand label, not for a better print surface. Classic tubular cotton tees from large basics brands are among the most printed blanks in Europe because they are consistent, come in many colours and suit bulk orders. Check the specs rather than the price.

How do I test a blank before a big print run?
Order a few pieces first, print one with your actual design and method, then wash it three times inside out at 30 to 40 °C. Check the print for cracking, the collar for waves and the body for twisting or shrinkage. A small 3-pack or 5-pack is a cheap way to test before ordering the full run.
